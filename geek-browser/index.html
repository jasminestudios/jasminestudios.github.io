Keyboard

Every page,
without leaving the keys.

Press f and every link gets a two-letter label — type it to open. o opens the address bar, : a command palette, / finds in the page. j k scroll, ⌘⇧] walks the tabs. If you know Vim or Vimlike, you already know Geek Browser.

  • Link hints f labels the links on screen; n / ⇧n step through them; Enter opens, ⇧Enter opens in a new tab. Point and press a to click whatever is under the cursor.
  • Command palette : lists every command with its key. Saved commands sit at the top, most recent first. :tab, :set darkmode=true, :javascript, :ping, and your own.
  • A help bar that teaches A one-line bar shows the keys for the mode you are in and lights up the one you pressed. Hide it when you know them.
  • Yours to remap Every key is a setting. Sequences like g g and y y work. Bind keys per website, or import your Vimlike settings.

Works with any hardware keyboard — Magic Keyboard, a Bluetooth keyboard, or the one on your Mac.

Agent

An assistant that works on the page —
and asks before it matters.

Type a question in the address bar and press Enter: the assistant answers from the page you are on. Give it a task and it opens a copy of the page in its own tab, marked ✦, and works through it step by step while you keep reading. Switch to that tab any time to watch or take over.

  • Reads, clicks, types Summarize, translate, extract a table, fill a form, follow links, open the five links that matter in background tabs. Each step shows in the side panel as it happens.
  • Three permission levels Read only never acts. Guard asks before leaving the site, submitting a form, buying or posting. Full access acts on its own. You choose, per run.
  • Knows your tabs Type @ to bring another open tab into the question. Select text and ask about it from the edit menu.
  • Learns the routine A saved command that worked once replays its clicks next time without the AI. If the site changed, the assistant takes over from that step and updates the routine.
  • Remembers what helps Plain markdown files — your preferences, notes per site, a log of each day's tasks. Open them, edit them, roll them back.
Passwords never reach the assistant. At a sign-in page it stops and hands you the tab; iCloud Keychain and passkeys fill there, as in Safari.
Private

Free to use.
Private by design.

There is no Geek Browser account and no Geek Browser server. History, bookmarks, settings and memory files stay on your iPad and in your own iCloud. AI runs where you decide.

  • On-device first Turn on “Reading tasks on this device” and summaries, explanations and translations run with Apple Intelligence on your iPad: free, offline, nothing sent.
  • Your key, your bill For tasks that act on pages, bring your own key for Claude, OpenAI, Gemini or DeepSeek. Keys stay in the Keychain. Usage and estimated cost show as the assistant works; set a budget per task.
  • Nothing to leak No analytics SDK in the app. No account. What the assistant sends is the page you are on, your question and the tabs you @-mention — to the provider you chose, and only then.
  • Ads and trackers, blocked A built-in content blocker, on by default, per site if you like.

Questions, answered.

Do I need a keyboard?

Geek Browser works by touch too, but it is designed for a hardware keyboard: Magic Keyboard, any Bluetooth keyboard, or a Mac's.

Does it run on my Mac?

Yes, on Apple silicon Macs as an iPad app from the Mac App Store, with the same keys.

Is it really free?

Yes. No in-app purchases, no subscription, no ads. AI on-device is free; cloud AI runs on your own key at your provider's prices.

Do I need an account or an API key?

No account, ever. AI reading tasks can run on-device with Apple Intelligence. For tasks that act on pages, add your own key for Claude, OpenAI, Gemini or DeepSeek — billed to you at cost.

What can the assistant do, and what will it never do?

It reads, scrolls, clicks, types, fills forms and opens tabs on your say-so. It never sees your passwords, never treats page text as instructions, never runs without a step limit, and stops the moment you press Esc. Settings › About lists it all.

Where is my data?

On your iPad and in your own iCloud: history, bookmarks, settings, saved commands, memory files. There is no Geek Browser server that stores them.

Can I use my Vimlike settings?

Yes. Export from Vimlike's Website Settings and import the file in Settings › Websites. Key names, site scripts and per-site commands come along.

What about passkeys and saved passwords?

Geek Browser is a real browser to iPadOS, so iCloud Keychain fills passwords and passkeys on any site. The assistant hands you the tab at sign-in pages.

Which languages?

English, Korean, Japanese, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Cantonese, German, French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese.
